# Level examples

One or two worked classifications per Bloom's level, to anchor judgments. These use CBSE
Class 10 material because that is this kit's worked example, but the reasoning pattern
carries to any curriculum.

If classifications keep coming out wrong for your curriculum (different question phrasing
conventions, different expectations per level), replace or extend these with 1-2 examples
per level from your own question bank and tell the classifier to follow yours. Examples
calibrate better than rules alone.

## Remembering

"Name the SI unit of force."
Level: Remembering (confidence 0.95). Pure recall of a memorized fact; nothing to work out.

"Who is the author of the poem 'Fire and Ice'?"
Level: Remembering (confidence 0.95). Recalling an author's name requires recognition only.

## Understanding

"Compare series and parallel combinations of resistors in terms of current flow."
Level: Understanding (confidence 0.75). "Compare" appears in both the Understanding and
Evaluating keyword lists, which is why confidence drops. The demand here is describing
learned differences, not judging worth, so Understanding wins.

"Summarize the main idea of the poem 'Dust of Snow'."
Level: Understanding (confidence 0.85). Restating a text's meaning in one's own words.

## Applying

"Calculate the resistance of an electric iron that draws 5 A from a 220 V supply."
Level: Applying (confidence 0.90). The student selects a known rule (V = IR) and uses it on
new numbers.

"Use the mirror formula to find the image distance for an object placed 20 cm in front of a
concave mirror of focal length 10 cm."
Level: Applying (confidence 0.90). Direct use of a formula in a structured situation.

## Analysing

"Sort the following processes into physical and chemical changes, giving one reason for
each choice."
Level: Analysing (confidence 0.80). Classifying items and justifying each placement means
breaking cases down against criteria.

"What does Valli's careful planning of her bus journey reveal about her character?"
Level: Analysing (confidence 0.80). The answer isn't stated in the text; the student infers
character traits from evidence.

## Evaluating

"Was Valli's decision to travel alone justified? Support your answer with evidence from the
story."
Level: Evaluating (confidence 0.85). The student takes a position and defends it. Note the
contrast with the Analysing example above: both are about Valli, but this one demands a
judgment, not just an inference.

## Creating

"Design an experiment to show that carbon dioxide is released during respiration."
Level: Creating (confidence 0.85). The student assembles known pieces into a new procedure.

"Write an alternative ending to the story in which Lencho discovers who really sent the
money."
Level: Creating (confidence 0.90). Producing an original composition from the source
material.
