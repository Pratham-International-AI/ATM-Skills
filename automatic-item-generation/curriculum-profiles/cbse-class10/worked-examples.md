# Worked examples (CBSE Class 10)

These are model questions that follow every rule in this profile. If you are an educator
reading this, they show you what "good" looks like. If you are the AI generating questions,
read them before you start: they show how detailed the marking points should be, how MCQ
options stay balanced, and how Science marking points carry their mark weights. Each one
ends with a short note on why it passes.

(When output is needed as JSON for a pipeline, the same content maps onto the fields in
`references/output-schemas.md`: question, options, answer, marks, blooms_level,
value_points.)

## Example 1. Science MCQ, 1 mark, Remembering

Question: Which of the following is the SI unit of electrical resistance?

- A. Ohm
- B. Volt
- C. Ampere
- D. Watt

Correct answer: A (Ohm)

Marking point: Ohm is the SI unit of resistance; from R = V/I, one ohm equals one volt per ampere.

Why it passes: all four options are single-word electrical units, so the correct answer
doesn't stand out. Every distractor is a real unit a student might confuse with resistance.
Simple recall suits the MCQ format.

## Example 2. Science fill-in-the-blank, 1 mark, Remembering

Question: The scattering of light by colloidal particles is called the _____.

Answer: Tyndall effect

Why it passes: the sentence is 10 words, under the 15-word limit. The whole term "Tyndall
effect" is blanked, not just "Tyndall" with "effect" left behind as a giveaway. Only one
answer fits.

## Example 3. Science short answer, 2 marks, Remembering

Question: State Ohm's law and write its mathematical expression.

Marking points:

- Current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it [½]
- Physical conditions such as temperature must remain constant [½]
- V = IR [½]
- V = potential difference, I = current, R = resistance [½]
- (Any other relevant point to be accepted)

Why it passes: four points at half a mark each add up to 2 marks. The formula is included,
each point stays under 15 words, and the list ends with the standard catch-all line.
Note the level: stating a memorized law is recall, so this is Remembering, not
Understanding, even though the word "state" sounds explanatory.

## Example 4. Science numerical, 2 marks, Applying

Question: A potential difference of 10 V produces a current of 2 A in a conductor. Calculate its resistance.

Marking points:

- Formula: R = V/I [½]
- Substitution: R = 10 V / 2 A [½]
- Calculation: R = 5 [½]
- Final answer with unit: 5 Ω [½]
- (Any other relevant point to be accepted)

Why it passes: this is the step-marks pattern for numericals. A student who picks the right
formula but slips on arithmetic still earns partial credit, and the unit is required in the
final step.

## Example 5. English MCQ, 1 mark, Remembering

From "Madam Rides the Bus":

Question: How did Valli feel when she saw the dead cow on her return journey?

- A. Sad
- B. Excited
- C. Angry
- D. Relieved

Correct answer: A (Sad)

Marking point: Valli was saddened; the cow she had seen running joyfully earlier now lay dead.

Why it passes: all options are single-word emotions of equal weight, so nothing gives the
answer away. Each distractor is a feeling a careless reader might pick.

## Example 6. English fill-in-the-blank, 1 mark, Remembering

Question: Valli saved _____ paise for her bus journey.

Answer: sixty

Why it passes: seven words, under the 10-word limit for English. The blank sits on a
keyword (a number from the chapter), and only one answer fits.

## Example 7. English short answer, 3 marks, Understanding

Question: Why did Valli refuse to get off the bus when it reached the town? (from the story "Madam Rides the Bus")

Marking points:

- Afraid to be alone in an unfamiliar town
- Had money only for the two-way fare
- Wanted only to ride and return
- (Any other relevant point to be accepted)

Why it passes: the points are short suggested phrases, not full sentences, because students
may word the ideas their own way. An examiner grades this as Content (2 marks, any two
points) plus Expression (1 mark), per this profile's marking rubric. The question calls the
source "the story", never "the text".
